# Planner decisions and defaults (Part 2)

Every default the shift planner relies on, with the reason and how to change it. Configuration paths refer to `config/planner.yaml` and `config/shift_patterns.yaml` (generated from `src/ree/planner/config.py` and `src/ree/planner/domain/patterns.py`; a test keeps the files equal to the code defaults). Parameters of the simulated store, the demand model and the queue simulator are illustrative assumptions, not facts about a real store. Labour rules are parameters to confirm with HR and the works council (`docs/labour_rules.md`). Where the method document, the store facts and Part 1 disagree, the reading chosen is stated in the entry concerned (P-04, P-06, P-09, P-11, P-23). Part 1 decisions stay in `docs/decisions.md`.

## Repository, dependencies and tooling

- **P-01 Files.** Planner decisions live here and Part 1 decisions in `docs/decisions.md`; the methods are in `docs/method_scoring.md` and `docs/method_planner.md`. Reason: each part keeps its own decisions next to its own method.
- **P-02 Python and solver.** `requires-python` stays `>=3.11`, which covers 3.12 and later (the dev machine runs 3.14, and OR-Tools 9.15 installs there). `ortools` is a runtime dependency; CP-SAT is the primary solver. The solver sits behind a `Solver` protocol; a heuristic solver (greedy plus local search) is the second implementation and the fallback. A `scipy.optimize.milp` (HiGHS) fallback was considered and is not needed because OR-Tools installs on Windows and in a fresh clone. Change: `solver.backend`.
- **P-03 Determinism.** Every random process takes its seed from `PlannerConfig.seed` through `ree.config.make_rng`. The solver uses a fixed `random_seed` and one worker by default; a deterministic time limit can replace the wall-clock limit when exact reproducibility matters. Change: `solver.*`.

## Calendar and store

- **P-04 Opening hours.** Monday to Saturday 08:30 to 21:30, Sunday 08:30 to 12:30 (as given for the store). Part 1's default store closes at 20:00 (Part 1 D-02); the planner has its own opening hours and reads only scores and hidden skills from Part 1 stores, so Part 1 is unchanged. Change: `opening.*`.
- **P-05 Rush windows.** Weekdays 10:30 to 14:00 and 18:00 to 20:00; Friday and Saturday evening rush to 21:00; Friday and Saturday 08:00 to 09:00 (clipped to the 08:30 opening, as in Part 1); Sunday 11:00 to 12:30. The last 30 minutes before the weekday close (21:00 to 21:30) are never rush; the Sunday rush runs to closing. On the 28th to the 4th of the month the weekday evening rush also runs to 21:00. Change: `rush.*`.
- **P-06 Weeks and cycle.** Weeks run Sunday to Saturday (as in the store's weekly rosters) and the roster cycle starts on a Sunday (default 2026-11-01); the store manager issues a roster every 3 weeks (the method document's first sections describe a weekly roster, its store answers a 3-week cycle, which wins). Change: `start_date`, `rules.planning_weeks`.
- **P-07 Seasons.** Arrival multipliers: month start (28th to 4th) 1.10; Christmas period (20 December to 3 January) 1.35 with a service time multiplier of 1.15 for the bigger baskets; late summer (25 August to 7 September) 1.15 with 1.05. The sizes are assumptions (the size of the month-start and Christmas effects is still an open question); a manager can override the arrival multiplier of a date. Change: `season.*`.
- **P-08 Grids.** Forecast and roster coverage use 30-minute slots; daily plans use a 5-minute grid; patterns are generated on a 5-minute grid and the solver pool is the 30-minute thinning of them (39 weekday and 3 Sunday patterns). Reason: the 5-minute pool has more than 1,100 weekday patterns, which a 3-week model of about 40 workers cannot carry. Change: `demand.slot_minutes`, `PatternSpec.solver_grid_minutes`.

## Shifts, breaks and rules

- **P-09 Patterns.** Generated from `config/shift_patterns.yaml`, not from a fixed list: morning blocks start 08:30 to 10:10 (paid 08:20 to 10:00) and end 12:55 to 16:15 (paid up to 16:20); evening blocks start 14:15 to 17:40 and end at the close (paid 21:35); a split family (9:30 or 9:40 start, first block ending about 13:30, a cut of 60 or 90 minutes, second block ending about 20:00); Sunday blocks start 08:30 to 09:30 and end at the close; Sunday patterns stay inside the opening hours (register end 12:30, paid end 12:35), although the sample rosters show Sunday paid windows to 12:55, which is unexplained and left as an open question, and the quiet last 30 minutes apply to the weekday close only. The method document's pattern section offers 6-hour continuous patterns every 30 minutes; its later store answers give start times on any 5-minute mark and longer ranges, which win. Register length 3 to 9 h 30 min in 5-minute steps, at most 10 paid hours. The `midday` family exists but is disabled (whether start times follow the rush windows is an open question). The generator is unit-tested against these ranges.
- **P-10 Margins.** Every block is paid from 10 minutes before the register start until 5 minutes after the register end; coverage counts register time only. A split shift clocks in and out per block. The checker rejects any block whose windows break the two margins.
- **P-11 Breaks.** Paid and counted as worked time. Length is 3 minutes per hour of register time in the block, rounded up to a multiple of 5 minutes (10 minutes at 3 hours, 15 at 4, 20 at 6, 30 at 9 h 30); blocks under 3 hours get none (the method document's fact table says a 20-minute break, its store answers 3 minutes per hour rounded up, which gives 20 at 6 hours and wins). The start window is centred on the middle of the block (after 3 hours in a 6-hour block) with a tolerance of 30 minutes. At most 2 workers on break at once, 3 only as a flagged limit case; the floor manager may move a break by at most 90 minutes (soft). Change: `rules.break_*`, `rules.max_simultaneous_breaks`.
- **P-12 Contract hours.** Weekly paid hours must stay within `rules.hours_tolerance_hours` (1.0) of the contract; nominal contracts are 15 and 21 hours for students and 30 or 35 for full-time workers. The weekly totals seen in the store's rosters (15 h 50 and 21 h 10 for students, 26 to 37 h for full-time) sit inside this tolerance. Change: `rules.hours_tolerance_hours`, `workforce.*`.
- **P-13 Burden.** Each pattern on a day carries a burden: closing 2, Sunday 3, Saturday 2, split 2, start before 09:00 1, Friday closing 1 more. Burden is compared per paid hour so that a full-time worker is not penalised for working more shifts. Change: `burden.*`.
- **P-14 Checker severities.** Rule violations that make a plan illegal are errors; coverage shortfalls and limit cases are warnings with their size. A solver roster must have zero errors; a shortfall caused by too few available workers is reported, not hidden.
- **P-15 Checker rules in detail.** Errors: the rules of `docs/labour_rules.md`, availability, patterns whose windows break the margins, a worker in two places, registers 1 and 4 not both staffed by register workers at any minute, breaks missing or outside the legal window by more than the soft tolerance, double-booked workers and posts, self-checkout-only workers on a register post, inconsistent handovers. Warnings: coverage below the requirement, too few self-checkout-cleared workers, a possible-absence window, a break moved inside the 90-minute tolerance, three people on break at once (limit case), people on duty minus people on break below the requirement, empty required posts. A solver roster must have zero errors.
- **P-16 Two rules with a definition worth knowing.** The no-open-and-close rule applies on days when the store is open longer than the daily maximum, because a 4-hour Sunday shift both opens and closes a 4-hour day. The weekly rest rule asks, per Sunday to Saturday week, for a gap of at least 35 hours between worked blocks of which a full day (35 minus the 11-hour daily rest) lies inside the week; the gap may extend into the neighbouring weeks and the edges of the roster horizon count as free time, because neighbouring cycles are planned separately.
- **P-17 Break moves.** A break start more than 30 minutes (the window tolerance) but at most 90 minutes from the middle of its block is reported as a `BREAK_MOVED` warning, farther is an error. The planner itself always plans inside the window.

## Demand, requirement and workforce

- **P-18 Demand model.** `lambda_hat(d, h) = L_w * P_w,h * S_d * U_d` on 30-minute slots. `L_w` is an exponentially weighted average (weight 0.35 on the newest week) of the same-weekday ticket totals divided by the seasonal multiplier; `P_w,h` is the pooled share of the day's customers per slot on days that are not month-start days, blended with the rush-window profile as a prior worth 30 customers (the profile alone is used until 3 such days exist); `S` is the product of the month-start, Christmas and late-summer multipliers; `U` is a manual uplift per date. On month-start weekdays the slot shares are re-weighted by the calendar so that the extended 20:00 to 21:00 rush shows up. Change: `demand.*`, `season.*`.
- **P-19 Planning quantile.** Slot counts follow a negative binomial with variance `mean + mean^2 / k`; `k` is estimated by moments from one-step-ahead forecast errors (default 60 without history). Rush slots plan at the 0.8 quantile, calm slots at 0.5. A rolling-origin backtest reports weighted absolute percentage error, bias and how often the planning quantile is exceeded against its nominal rate. Without real tickets the simulated arrival process is the ground truth and the report says so. Change: `demand.quantile_*`, `demand.dispersion_k`.
- **P-20 Default demand level.** Expected register tickets per day: Monday 1,300, Tuesday 1,250, Wednesday 1,400, Thursday 1,450, Friday 1,800, Saturday 2,000, Sunday 460; rush slots are 2.2 times as busy as calm slots and the first and last calm slot of a day 0.6 times. These are assumptions chosen so that the Erlang requirement opens 7 to 9 registers in the rush (the store opens 8 to 9 today) and so that a first week needs 944 register hours, which the default workforce of 40 covers at about 92 percent of its contract hours (asserted by tests). Change: `demand.daily_customers`.
- **P-21 Service time and targets.** Occupancy of a register per customer is 2 minutes (the method document's worked example), multiplied by the seasonal service factor and an optional safety multiplier; `estimate_service_minutes` shows how to derive it from the median gap between transaction starts at a register that never idles. The wait target is 3 minutes with `alpha` 0.20 in rush slots and 0.35 in calm slots; the 5-customers-per-register queue limit is converted with `queue_limit * s / open registers` for reporting and the queue simulator reports queue length. Change: `staffing.*`.
- **P-22 Headcount.** `N = max(c, 2) + k_sc + beta` with `k_sc` 3 in calm and 5 in rush slots (zone 1: 1 and 2, zone 2: 2 and 3) and `beta = max(floaters, ceil(0.06 * (c + k_sc)))` where floaters are 2 before 14:00 and 2 after. Floaters are posts held by roster workers who are on duty without a required post; they are the break buffer. The requirement is capped at 15 registers and flagged `saturated` when even 15 cannot meet the target.
- **P-23 Workforce size.** The planner's staff are the cashiers of a Part 1 simulated store, 40 of them by default (a pool of 25 cashiers cannot staff the requirement: 8 or 9 registers, 5 supervisors and 2 floaters in the rush need about 940 register hours a week against about 700 contract hours for 25 cashiers; floaters and supervisors are posts held by cashiers and need Part 1 scores for the relief rule, so the 25 plus about 15 who hold those posts are modelled as one pool of 40). Contracts: 45 percent students at 15 or 21 hours, the rest full-time at 35 or 30 hours. A scenario "small team" with 25 workers shows the capacity-limited case. Change: `workforce.*`.

## Roster solver

- **P-24 Roster model.** One boolean per worker, day and pattern from the solver pool, none for patterns that touch a blocked window. Hard: one pattern per day, weekly paid hours within `rules.hours_tolerance_hours` of the contract, the minimum rest between consecutive days (written as cliques over the patterns that end late and the patterns that start early), at most `max_consecutive_days`, a weekly rest, and at least two register-capable workers on a register in every slot. Soft, in the objective: understaffing (priced higher in rush slots), overstaffing, register shortage, self-checkout shortage, the score reward (off by default, P-50), burden spread, the tier guard, the learning quota, soft availability and stability. Change: `objective.*`, `rules.*`.
- **P-25 Score input.** `z = clip((score - 50) / 15, -2, 2) * confidence`, with the peak score, tier and confidence of Part 1 (the method document says a wide interval shrinks the score toward 0; Part 1's confidence is that shrinkage). Provisional, unrated and unknown workers get `z = 0`, tier "unrated" and a learning flag. The reward is `score_weight * score_scale * z * pressure` where `pressure` is the rush weight of the slots the pattern covers; slots outside rush have weight 0, so scores never move a worker in or out of the roster, only between shifts. The reward is off by default (`objective.score_weight` 0) because it buys hours (P-50). Change: `objective.score_*`.
- **P-26 Determinism.** The default `reproducible` mode runs 8 interleaved workers with `max_deterministic_time` and a wall-clock guard of 60 times the budget; results are identical for identical inputs (checked for 50 seeds under two different machine loads). `fast` uses a wall-clock limit and is not bit-reproducible. A single worker and heuristic hints were tried and were clearly worse. The default budget is 60 deterministic seconds; the 10-worker test store uses 4. Change: `solver.*`.
- **P-27 Relaxation and fallback.** If no roster exists with every rule, the solver turns the register floor into a penalty, then relaxes the weekly rest, then widens the contract tolerance threefold; if even that fails it falls back to the constructive heuristic. The solution lists the rule groups that gave way and the checker report names every violation that remains.
- **P-28 Tier guard.** The average burden per paid hour of each score tier may differ from the overall average by `objective.tier_epsilon` (35 percent). It is a priced term, not a hard constraint, because a hard guard made the first feasible roster hard to find. After the solve, while the gap is above the limit, a repair step swaps patterns between workers of different tiers on the same day (same role flags, equal paid minutes, both workers legal, coverage unchanged) and keeps the swap that lowers the gap most; it never widens the gap and stops when no swap helps. The roster gate reports the largest gap on the 10-worker test store (48.7 percent, not a criterion there: its top and bottom tiers have 2 workers each, so a chance gap of that size is expected); the audit and the evaluation check the limit on the 24-worker store, and in scenarios with too few people for the requirement the guard can still be exceeded, which the evaluation report counts. Change: `objective.tier_epsilon`, `objective.tier_penalty`.
- **P-29 Score refinement.** After the solve, pairs of same-day patterns are swapped between workers with the same role flags when the swap raises the sum of `z * pressure`, the two patterns have the same paid minutes and the same burden on that day, and both workers stay legal (checked with the checker's worker rules). Neither worker's weekly paid minutes nor burden change, so the step only decides which of several equivalent shifts a person takes, and it never changes who works on a day. The first version swapped patterns of any length and burden inside the tolerance and the guard; it is kept as `objective.neutral_swaps: false`, the negative control of the audit (P-50).
- **P-30 Evaluation scale.** The roster tests and the 50-scenario gate use the 10-worker "tiny" store (demand at 15 percent of the default, one self-checkout supervisor in the rush, no floaters), one week, so that 50 solves take about three minutes. The 24-worker "medium" store is the one the evaluation uses; the 40-worker default store is used for scale checks because the deterministic interleaved search is weak there.

## Daily plan

- **P-31 Break scheduling.** One CP-SAT model per day with a single worker (deterministic, budget 20 deterministic seconds). Each break has one start on the 5-minute grid inside the legal window of its block (rules: length 3 minutes per hour rounded up to 5, window centred on the middle of the block, tolerance 30 minutes). Priced terms: capacity slack against `registers + k_sc` (100 per person and 5 minutes; floaters are the buffer and are not counted as required), more than 2 at once (40) and more than 3 at once (1,000), break cells in rush slots (3), two breaks starting at the same minute (2) and distance from the middle of the block (1 per step). The window is never widened by the planner; a break the manager moves is revalidated by the checker. Change: `daily.break_*`.
- **P-32 Post assignment.** One Hungarian assignment per 5-minute slot. Columns are the required posts in priority order (truncated when fewer workers are free than posts) plus one floater column per worker; a fill bonus decreasing with priority makes the solver fill posts first. Cost of a worker at a post: `-theta * omega * z * importance` plus `sigma` when the worker leaves a post they held in the previous slot, minus or plus `preferred_sco_bonus` for workers with the soft self-checkout preference; forbidden pairs (self-checkout-only worker at a register, worker without clearance at a self-checkout post) cost 10 million. `importance` is 1.0 for registers 1 and 4, 0.8 for the rest of the first 7 registers, 0.4 for the others and 0.6 for self-checkout posts; `omega` is 1 in rush slots and 0.25 otherwise; `theta` 6 and `sigma` 2. Unrated and provisional workers have `z = 0`. Change: `daily.*`.
- **P-33 Relief chain.** The relief list is derived from the final timeline, not planned separately: whoever takes a post at the minute its holder starts a break gets a `relief` handover that names the replaced worker; a worker back from a break is `return`, a post that nobody held a slot earlier is `opening`, a worker going from a post to floating is `release`, any other change is `rotation`. Because a floater has no post to lose, the continuity cost makes the floater the natural replacement, and the worker who comes back becomes a floater or covers the next break, which is the chain described in the method document.
- **P-34 What the daily plan does not do.** It does not pair provisional workers with a tier A colleague (the roster layer gives them peak slots; the pairing at the register is left for a later version), it is myopic from slot to slot, and the joint CP-SAT version is not built.

## Evaluation, live re-planning, service and screens

- **P-35 Evaluation store.** The evaluation uses a store of 24 cashiers with demand at 60 percent of the default, one self-checkout supervisor in calm slots and two in the rush, and one floater. Its requirement is about 80 percent of the contract hours (518 against 646), which leaves the roster solver room to cover the forecast. The 10 and 16-worker stores were tried first: at 16 workers the requirement used 98 percent of the contract hours, so no plan could cover its target, and the full 40-worker store is too hard for the deterministic search in the time available. The store size is a parameter (`planner evaluate --store`).
- **P-36 Workforce and hidden truth.** Each seed simulates a Part 1 store with the same number of cashiers for 8 weeks and scores it with the Part 1 engine (60 bootstrap replicates). The planner receives only the scores, intervals and tiers through `ScoreProvider`. The hidden true log-speed (with the newcomer deficit decayed by tenure) and the peak effect are used by the queue simulator and by the oracle plan D, and nowhere else. Contracts and availability come from the planner's own workforce generator with the same worker ids.
- **P-37 Queue simulator.** Event-driven. Customers join the shortest line among the open registers (queue length plus the customer in service) plus 0.15 per position in the opening order, so that registers 1 and 4 attract more customers; this preference is what makes it worthwhile to put the fastest cashiers there and the report shows the result without it and with a stronger one. Service time is 40 seconds fixed plus 80 seconds of work (lognormal, coefficient of variation 0.6, multiplied by the seasonal factor) times `exp(-speed)` of the cashier at the register; patience is exponential with a mean of 8 minutes; a register that closes finishes its customer and its queue moves to the other registers; at closing time the open registers stay open until their lines are empty. Self-checkout customers are not simulated. A stationary run with exponential service and a shared line reproduces Erlang C within 2 percent (tests and `planner queue-check`); one line per register waits 11 to 73 percent longer than Erlang C, the more registers the larger the gap.
- **P-38 Plans.** A: the number of registers is the weekly average of the demand-aware requirement, rounded, for every rush slot and for every calm slot (same self-checkout and floater counts). B: the demand-aware requirement. C: B with the Part 1 scores in the roster (equal-hours, equal-burden swaps, the tier guard and the learning quota; P-50) and in the posts. D: B with the standardised hidden true speeds as scores. E: B with the Part 1 scores shuffled across workers. Plans B to E are solved coverage first (stage one on a copy of the problem without scores, so the four share one coverage solution; stage two under that coverage, with every worker's weekly paid minutes fixed to those of stage one, with the tier guard and the learning quota); without it the search noise between solves was larger than the effect being measured.
- **P-39 Scenarios and statistics.** Six scenarios (normal week, Saturday surge of 30 percent, 8 percent of shifts missed, the week from 20 December, a random 15 percent error of the demand level, a quarter of the cashiers missing). All plans face the same customers (common random numbers); each seed repeats the week 5 times and averages; the unit of replication is the seed (8 seeds) and the intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals of the paired differences over seeds (2,000 resamples). The roster is solved once per seed for each plan and shared by the scenarios that only change the world.
- **P-40 Metrics.** Wait counts customers who left without service (until they left). The share over target counts customers who waited more than 3 minutes or left. Also reported: the 90th and 95th percentile, abandonment, idle share of the open registers, queue per open register, time with a queue above 5 per register, registers staffed, uncovered staff time, breaks that leave people below capacity, handovers per shift, burden spread, tier gap and paid hours.
- **P-41 Live re-planning.** Every 5 minutes the busyness of the open registers (busy time over open time) is read. If it stays above 90 percent for 10 minutes the planner proposes that the best-scoring floater opens the next register in the opening order from 10 minutes ahead; if it stays below 55 percent for 20 minutes with more than one floater it proposes closing the register that was opened last (never below 2); a worker who has not logged in 10 minutes after the start is covered by floaters; a manager event re-solves the posts for the rest of the day with a penalty of 5 for each assignment that differs from the plan, the past and the next 10 minutes frozen. The same kind of proposal comes at most once in 10 minutes, a close is not proposed within 20 minutes of an open, and a proposal that would introduce a rule error is shown as blocked. Expected waits with and without the change come from Erlang C with the arrival rate blended 50-50 with the forecast. Change: `live.*`.
- **P-42 Nothing is applied without acceptance.** A proposal is a card with a reason, a cost (people affected) and a state. Accepting one applies it to the day, makes the other pending proposals stale and writes to the audit log; rejecting keeps the plan and stores the manager's short note. In the evaluation plan L has a simulated manager who accepts every proposal, which is the most favourable assumption about how often the advice is followed.
- **P-43 Service.** `PlannerService` (roster, daily plans, checks, overrides, live proposals, worker view) is shared by the FastAPI app and the Streamlit screens. State is in memory (a restart loses the current roster); the audit log is an SQLite table. An override needs a reason of at least 3 characters and is applied only if it introduces no rule error; a manual break move is rechecked the same way. Scores, tiers, hidden truth and the reasons behind availability or self-checkout flags are never returned, and the worker screen shows reasons in words without ranks. The roster for the demo comes from the simulator (`planner/sim/demo.py`), which returns only workers, requirement and a score provider.
- **P-44 Fairness audit.** `planner audit` solves, for each seed, plans B, C and E, plan C with normal noise of 0.25, 0.5 and 1.0 (in z units) on the scores, and plan C without the learning quota, and reports tier gaps, hours deviations, paid-hours differences against plan B, correlations of score with weekend, late and split shares and with burden, the correlation of the number of blocked days with burden, the rush minutes of new workers and how many of plan C's shifts survive. It also reports how many worker-days and how many shifts of the same worker-day differ from plan B, which shows what the scores change (not the days people work, only which shift of the day), and `planner audit --report-only` rebuilds the report from the saved table.
- **P-45 Ablation plan F.** F keeps the roster of plan B (no scores) and uses the Part 1 scores only to choose who stands at which register in the daily plan. It is built from B's bundle (no extra roster solve), simulated in every scenario with the same customers as the other plans, and has its own section in the report ("Where the benefit of the scores comes from"). It is not one of the five plans A to E of the method document or part of the placebo test, and appears only in the staffing table, the alignment figure and its own section; it exists to separate what the scores add in the posts from what they add in the roster.
- **P-46 Posts-only mode.** `objective.scores_in_roster` (default true) makes `plan_roster` replace the scores by neutral ones, so the roster ignores them and the daily plan, which receives the real scores separately, still uses them. The service gives the reasons and the trade-offs the same neutral inputs, so no reason says that a score placed someone, and adds a note to the roster. Switches: `--posts-only` on `planner roster` and `planner daily`, `posts_only` in `POST /roster/generate`, a checkbox in the manager screen. The default stays true because the method document describes the full planner. After P-50 the default and posts-only mode give the same roster in the evaluated runs (the audit's plan C equals plan B), so the switch keeps the scores out of the roster by configuration, which is the simplest thing to explain to a works council, and not because the default is unsafe.
- **P-47 Queue length and idle capacity.** The store's tolerance is a queue limit, so the report gives queue length as well as wait. The report adds a table with the customers waiting per open register (time average), the share of the open day in which some open register had more than 5 customers waiting (the manager's limit, `service.queue_limit_customers`) and the idle share of open-register time.
- **P-48 Reading text computed from the verdicts.** The live statement, the placebo statement, the ablation statement and the audit reading are generated from the tables and say so when a result is mixed, absent or the wrong way round (tests with crafted frames). Where the live plan is better in one scenario and worse in another the text names both.
- **P-49 Stability.** The stability term (15 per changed assignment) is in the model and the metric is a tested function. `planner stability` measures the effect: it blocks two worker-days of a solved roster and solves again with and without the previous roster as reference. The reference removes under half of the changes (47 percent in `outputs/planner/reports/stability.md`): at this search budget the solver does not stay near the previous roster. A warm start from the previous roster with hint repair was tried on a few probe seeds and abandoned, because the search then fell back to the heuristic and returned rosters with rule errors. A repair mode that fixes everything outside a neighbourhood of the change is not built. The evaluation does not measure stability, because it plans one week from scratch.
- **P-50 Scores never buy hours.** The first version of the solver, audited on 5 October 2026, showed in plan C of the 24-worker store a Spearman correlation of +0.51 between the score and the signed deviation of paid hours from the contract (tier A paid 1.27 h a week more than tier C against their contracts, all inside the 1-hour tolerance) and of -0.35 between the score and the burden per paid hour (24 workers, 6 seeds). A staged measurement attributed all of it to the score refinement step (without that step the figures equalled plan B's), and a 24-seed probe of the single-stage model showed that its score reward buys hours too, because the reward grows with the length of the pattern. Changes: (1) the CP-SAT reward is off by default (`objective.score_weight` 0); (2) refinement swaps only patterns with equal paid minutes and equal burden on the day (`objective.neutral_swaps`, default true); (3) the repair step swaps only patterns with equal paid minutes; (4) in coverage-first solving the second stage fixes every worker's weekly paid minutes to those of the score-blind first stage; (5) coverage-first is the default for every store except the 10-worker test store, because the single-stage model cannot be made hours-neutral (the tier guard and the quota use tiers and flags) and is weak at scale anyway. Consequences: in coverage-first mode every worker's weekly paid minutes are the same for every score vector, including a swap of two workers' scores (tests), and the audit checks that they equal plan B's; the tier guard acts only through equal-hours swaps, so on small stores a chance gap above the limit can remain (the roster summary and the screens show it); the audit keeps the old swaps as a negative control that must show the leak. The method document states the principle as scores never changing hours or burden, but implements the burden side as a tolerance guard (a limit, not an equality) plus an audit of the correlation between score and burden, and puts a reward on the score in the roster objective; P-50 removes the reward and the hours leak, and the guard stays a limit.
